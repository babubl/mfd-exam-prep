    About the exam

    NISM, the National Institute of Securities Markets, runs certification exams under the SEBI (Certification of Associated Persons in the Securities Markets) Regulations, 2007. Series V-A sets the common minimum knowledge for everyone who sells mutual funds.

    Who needs it

    • Individual mutual fund distributors
    • Employees of firms that sell and distribute mutual funds, such as banks and distribution companies
    • AMC employees, especially those in sales and distribution

    What you'll be able to do

    • Explain how mutual funds work, how they are structured and how schemes differ
    • Evaluate schemes and recommend suitable products to investors
    • Handle the legal, accounting, valuation and tax side of mutual funds

    From exam to your first client

    The workbook (Chapter 6) sets out these steps for an individual distributor.

    1. Pass NISM Series V-A

      This is mandatory for anyone who sells or distributes mutual fund products.

    2. Complete Know Your Distributor (KYD)

      Submit a self-attested PAN and address proof at a CAMS Point of Service, show the originals, and give a biometric impression of your right index finger. You'll get an acknowledgement.

    3. Get your ARN from AMFI

      AMFI allots an AMFI Registration Number (ARN). You must be at least 18, and since 1 October 2024 applications are online only, through amfiindia.com.

    4. Empanel with AMCs

      With your ARN you can empanel with any number of fund houses, or work as an agent under a distributor who is already empanelled. You can't earn commission without one of these.

    5. Start serving investors

      Quote your ARN and EUIN on every Regular Plan transaction, follow the AMFI code of conduct, and renew your NISM certificate and AMFI registration on time.

    Joining a bank or distribution firm? You still pass V-A, then get an Employee Unique Identification Number (EUIN) from AMFI and work under the firm's ARN.

    Firms that sell mutual funds can also offer Specialized Investment Funds (SIFs), if they meet SEBI's extra eligibility rules.

    How distributors earn

    Distributors are paid trail commission: a percentage of the client's money, worked out on daily net assets and paid monthly or quarterly. Unlike insurance, where agent commission stops after a few years, trail continues for as long as the client stays invested.

    SEBI now allows only trail commission, paid in money. Upfront commission and transaction charges have been withdrawn.

    AMCs also pay an extra commission for bringing in new investors (a new PAN) from smaller towns beyond the top 30 cities, and new women investors anywhere. It's 1% of the first lump sum or of the first year's SIPs, up to Rs 2,000.
