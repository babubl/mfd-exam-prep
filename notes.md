## How to use these notes

Target 90 of 100: the exam is 100 one-mark questions in 2 hours, pass mark 50, no negative marking, so never leave a question blank. These notes follow the March 2026 revised workbook, which reflects the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026 and the new expense limits from 1 April 2026.

**What the real paper feels like.** A candidate who sat the exam in October 2026 found it harder than recall drills suggest. About **20 to 30 questions** gave two or three statements and asked which is true or which is false. **Fewer than 10** needed any calculation. Several questions were "tweaks" that test whether you understand a concept, such as asking what the **numerator** of a ratio represents rather than its value. Learn each rule well enough to spot a near-miss: a swapped number, a swapped body (trustees vs AMC), or an added "only". Each chapter now ends with **Exam traps**, and Practice has a **Statement drill**.

**Big changes in the March 2026 workbook.** Know these cold:

- The **SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026** replace the 1996 Regulations. Schedule numbers moved: valuation = **Seventh**, ad code = **Fifth**, code of conduct = **Fourth**, investment restrictions = **Sixth**.
- New expense limits (**base expense ratio**): equity **2.10%** and non-equity **1.85%** on the first Rs 500 cr; index/ETF **0.90%**; brokerage **0.06% / 0.02%**; the extra **0.05% is gone**.
- Categories: equity **13**, debt **17**, hybrid **7**; **Life Cycle Funds** replace solution-oriented (retirement/children's) funds.
- Distributors earn **trail commission only**. Upfront commission and transaction charges were withdrawn by SEBI in August 2025.
- Investors may name **up to 10 nominees**.

## Chapter 1 – Investment Landscape (8 marks)

#### Basics

| Point | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Financial goal | Objective + amount + timeline = **financial goal** |
| Hospitalisation, theft, fire | Cover with **emergency fund + insurance**, not investments |
| Pool approach | Flaw: **horizon unknown** |
| Saving vs investing | **Saving precedes investing**; "saving" comes from "safe" |
| Mutual fund | A **different way of investing**, not a different product |

#### Compounding examples

| Example | Horizon | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shalini: Rs 50 lakh at 8% | 10 years | **Rs 1,07,94,625** |
| Rs 30,000 at 6% | 1 yr | **31,800** |
| Rs 30,000 at 6% | 10 yrs | **53,725** |
| Rs 30,000 at 6% | 20 yrs | **96,214** |
| Rs 10,000 at 8% | 5 yrs | **14,693** |
| Rs 10,000 at 8% | 10 yrs | **21,589** |
| Rs 10,000 at 8% | 20 yrs | **46,610** |
| Rs 10,000 at 8% | 30 yrs | **1,00,627** |

- At 8%: 100 units buys **68** after 5 yrs, **46** after 10.

#### Evaluating an investment

| Point | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Top 3 evaluation factors | **safety, liquidity, returns** |
| Exit penalty | Hits **returns**, not liquidity |
| **Nominal** | Before inflation |
| **real** | After inflation |
| Listed equity | Liquid but **unfit for short-term needs** |

- Four asset classes: **Real estate, Commodities, Equity, Fixed income**.
- Only financial form: **equity and bonds**.

#### Asset class features

| Asset class | Key points |
| --- | --- |
| Real estate | **location** is key; illiquid; **not divisible**; self-occupied home = **expense** |
| Commodities | Only **commodities** give no intermittent cash flow; physical gold = no regular income |
| Commodity derivatives | Aren't investments: **leveraged + short-term** |
| Bonds | Bought at issue, held to maturity: **no capital gain** |
| Sensex | Base **100 in 1979**, **82,269 by 30 Jan 2026**, **\~15.4% p.a.** (before dividends) |

#### Table 1.1: where products fit

| Product | Asset class |
| --- | --- |
| **PPF, SSY, SCSS, POMIS, endowment/money-back** | **Fixed Income** |
| **REITs, InvITs, REMF** | **Real Estate** |
| **coins, art, stamps** | **Others** |

#### Types of risk and how they are measured

| Risk | Key point |
| --- | --- |
| Credit risk | Causes: borrower's **ability or intention**; measured by **rating + spreads** |
| Own government's bonds | **safest, lowest rate** |
| Pager industry | **industry-specific** risk |
| Market-wide vs company-specific | **Market-wide risk cannot be diversified**; company-specific can |
| Rates vs bond prices | Rates up, bond price **down**; **longer maturity = bigger swing** |
| Volatility | Measured by **variance, SD, beta, modified duration** |
| Risk strategies | **avoid, take a position, diversify**; lay investor = **diversify** |

#### Behavioural biases

| Behaviour | Name |
| --- | --- |
| Dominant emotions | **fear, greed, hope** |
| Rs 5,000 loss hurts more than gain | **loss aversion** (Kahneman & Tversky) |
| Decide first, seek proof later | **confirmation bias** |
| "Known devil vs unknown angel" | **familiarity bias** |
| Bull run drives over-allocation | **recency bias** |
| IT employee with only tech stocks | **interest of the investor** |
| Remedy for biases | Third opinion from an **RIA or MFD** |

#### Risk profile

| Element | Made of |
| --- | --- |
| Risk profile | **need + ability + willingness** |
| Ability | **finances + horizon** |
| Willingness | **psychology** |

#### Allocation and rebalancing

| Approach | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Strategic | **fixed target mix** |
| Tactical/dynamic | **improve risk-adjusted return**; for **seasoned investors with large surplus** |
| Rebalancing | **sell what rose, buy what fell** |
| Rebalancing example | 50:50 with A +20%, B −5% on Rs 1,000 each: **sell Rs 125 of A** |
| Passive breach rebalancing | **30 business days**; excludes index funds/ETFs; **not applicable to overnight funds** |

- DIY hidden costs: **time + mistakes**; 2% fee on Rs 10 lakh = **\~Rs 20,000/yr**.

### Exam traps

- An exit penalty hurts **returns**, not liquidity.
- **Market-wide** risk cannot be diversified; only company-specific risk can.
- Ability to take risk = **finances + horizon**; willingness = **psychology**, not finances.
- Deciding first and seeking proof later = **confirmation** bias; 'known devil vs unknown angel' = **familiarity** bias; over-allocating after a bull run = **recency** bias.
- A self-occupied home is an **expense**, not an investment.
- REITs, InvITs and REMF = **Real Estate**, not equity; PPF, SSY, SCSS, POMIS and endowment policies = **Fixed Income**; coins, art, stamps = **Others**.
- **Commodities** are the only asset class with no intermittent cash flow, not fixed income.
- Credit risk is measured by **rating and spreads**; variance, SD, beta and modified duration measure **volatility**.

## Chapter 2 – Concept and Role of a Mutual Fund (6 marks)

- You invest **through** a mutual fund, not **in** one; it is a **different way of investing**, not a different product.
- A mutual fund is set up as a **trust**. Profits and losses belong **only to unitholders**; everyone else earns a **fee or commission**.
- Each separate pool of money = a **scheme**, with a pre-announced **investment objective**. Investment policy = **asset allocation + investment style**, disclosed in the **SID**.
- MFs act as a **market stabiliser**: in **2022 and 2025** MF inflows countered heavy **FPI** selling.
- MF is a **pass-through vehicle**; the fund manager controls only **his own skill**, not the market or securities.

#### Key terms

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Face value | Typically **Rs 10** (can differ, e.g. **Rs 100**) |
| Unit capital | **Units × face value** |
| AUM | **NAV × units outstanding**; AMCs are ranked by **AUM** |
| NAV | **Net realisable value per unit** if the scheme were liquidated |
| Mark to market | Valuing each security at market price, **daily** |
| IDCW | Old "dividend" option, now **Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal** |
| Recurring expenses | Charged as a **% of AUM**; they **lower the NAV** |

#### Advantages and limitations

| Advantages | Limitations |
| --- | --- |
| Diversification from as little as **Rs 500** | **No customisation** (PMS gives it) |
| Transparency: **SID, SAI, KIM + portfolio + NAV**, open to **prospective investors** too | **Choice overload** (SEBI **categorisation** was the answer) |
| **Tax deferral**: MFs don't pay tax on income; growth option defers tax | **No control over costs** |
| **ELSS**: up to **Rs 1,50,000** under **Sec 80C**, **Old Tax Regime** only | **No guaranteed returns** |
| **SIP** (invest), **SWP** (withdraw), **STP** (transfer) | |

#### Open-ended, close-ended, interval and ETF

| Structure | Buying and selling | Key points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open-ended | Buy or sell **any time after the NFO** | Unit capital **keeps changing** |
| Close-ended | Buy **only in the NFO**; exit through the exchange | **Fixed maturity**; **compulsorily listed** (**except ELSS**); unit capital **fixed**; usually trades at a **discount to NAV** (the buyer has the bargaining power) |
| Interval | Close-ended, but **opens at intervals** | **Listed**; transaction period **min 2 days**; interval period **15 days** (workbook wording) |
| ETF | Trades **all day** at multiple prices; needs a **demat account** | Tracks NAV closely; **Silver ETF** now allowed |

#### Active vs passive

| | Active | Passive (index / ETF) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aim | Beat the market | Track the index |
| Cost | Higher | **Low** |
| Fund manager's choice | Yes | No |

**MF Lite** = **Chapter X** of the MF Regulations 2026: a lighter framework for **passive** schemes only (index funds, ETFs, FoFs).

#### SEBI categorisation (2026 circular)

| Category | Number of schemes |
| --- | --- |
| Equity | **13** |
| Debt | **17** |
| Hybrid | **7** |
| Life Cycle Funds | New category |
| Other | **2** |

- Market cap: **Large 1–100**, **Mid 101–250**, **Small 251+**, by **full market cap**.
- One scheme per category, **except**: index funds/ETFs on different indices, FoFs with different underlyings, sector/thematic funds on different sectors.

#### Equity schemes: mandate

| Category | Minimum in equity / rule |
| --- | --- |
| Multi cap | **≥75%** equity, with **≥25% each** in large, mid and small |
| Large cap | **80%** in large caps |
| Large & Mid cap | **35% + 35%** |
| Mid cap | **65%** in mid caps |
| Small cap | **65%** in small caps |
| Dividend yield | **80%** |
| Value | **80%**; separate from contra |
| Contra | **80%**; an AMC can offer both value and contra if portfolio overlap is **≤50%** |
| Focused | **Max 30 stocks**, **80%** equity |
| Sectoral | **80%**; separate from thematic |
| Thematic | **80%**; overlap with other sectoral/thematic and equity schemes **≤50%** (**except large cap**) |
| ELSS – Tax Saver Fund | **80%** + **3-year lock-in** |
| Flexi cap | **65%**, any mix of market caps |

Equity schemes may put the **residual** in money market, **gold and silver** instruments and **InvITs**.

#### Debt schemes: mandate

| Category | Rule (duration = Macaulay) |
| --- | --- |
| Overnight | **1 day**; may put **≤5%** in G-secs/T-bills (**≤30 days**) for margin |
| Liquid | Up to **91 days** |
| Ultra-Short-Term | **3–6 months** |
| Ultra Short to Short Term (was low duration) | **6–12 months** |
| Money market | Up to **1 year** |
| Short Term | **1–3 years** |
| Medium Term | **3–4 years** |
| Medium to Long Term | **4–7 years** |
| Long Term | **> 7 years** |
| Dynamic Term | **Any** duration |
| Corporate bond | **80% in AA+ and above** |
| Credit risk | **65% in AA and below** (AA+ excluded); name can't stress return (**no "high yield", "credit opportunities"**) |
| Banking and PSU Debt | **80%** |
| Gilt | **80%** in G-secs |
| 10-year constant maturity Gilt | **80%** in G-secs, duration **= 10 years** |
| Floating Interest Rates Fund | **65%** |
| Sectoral (debt) Fund | **80%** in one sector, **only AA+ and above**; Financial Services, Energy, Infrastructure, Housing, Real Estate |

#### Hybrid schemes: mandate

| Category | Mandate |
| --- | --- |
| Conservative hybrid | Debt **75–90%**, equity **10–25%** |
| Balanced hybrid | Equity **40–60%**, **no arbitrage** |
| Aggressive hybrid | Equity **65–80%** |
| Balanced Advantage | **Dynamic** equity/debt |
| Multi asset allocation | **≥3 asset classes**, **≥10% each**; **foreign securities are not a separate class** |
| Arbitrage | **65%** equity; debt only in **G-secs under 1 year** and **G-sec repo** |
| Equity savings | **65%** equity + **10%** debt; **net equity 15–40%** |

- The category is now "Balanced Hybrid **and** Aggressive Hybrid".
- Hybrid schemes may put the **residual** in **InvITs** (not arbitrage funds), **ETCDs, gold and silver ETFs**.

#### How hybrid funds are taxed (from Chapter 8)

| Hybrid | Usual tax treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Conservative hybrid | Debt-oriented: **slab rate**, whatever the holding period |
| Balanced hybrid | 35–65% equity: **slab** up to 24 months, **12.5%** after 24 months |
| Aggressive hybrid | Equity-oriented: **20%** up to 12 months, **12.5%** after; **Rs 1.25 lakh** exempt a year |
| Arbitrage | Equity-oriented: **20% / 12.5%** after 12 months |
| Equity savings | Equity-oriented: **20% / 12.5%** after 12 months |
| Balanced Advantage, Multi asset | **Depends on the actual equity share**: above 65% = equity rules |

Tax follows the scheme's equity share: **more than 65% listed Indian equity** = equity-oriented; **35–65%** = slab, then 12.5% after 24 months; **more than 65% debt** = slab rate.

#### Life Cycle Funds

- **Glide path** across equity, debt, InvITs, ETCDs, gold and silver ETFs: equity falls, debt rises as maturity nears.
- Tenure **5–30 years** in **multiples of 5**; max **6** open for subscription per MF at a time.

| Years to maturity (15-year fund) | Equity | Debt | Gold, silver, ETCD, InvIT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10–15 | **65–80%** | 5–25% | 0–10% |
| 5–10 | **50–65%** | 5–25% | |
| 3–5 | **35–50%** | 25–50% | |
| 1–3 | **20–35%** | 25–65% | |
| Under 1 | **5–20%** | 25–65% | |

#### Other schemes (2) and other fund types

| Type | Key feature |
| --- | --- |
| Index fund / ETF | **95%** in index securities |
| Fund of funds (FoF) | **95%** in the underlying fund |
| FMP | Close-ended debt; portfolio matches maturity; returns clearer but **not guaranteed** |
| Target Maturity Fund | Matures on a set date (**2–14 years**), **listed**, reduces **reinvestment risk** |
| Capital protection fund | Close-ended hybrid; e.g. **Rs 90 in debt grows to Rs 100**, rest in **equity derivatives** |
| IDF | Close-ended, **≥90%** in infra debt; IDF-MF sponsored by **banks and NBFCs**; IDF-NBFC by **banks and IFCs** only |
| REMF | Real estate in cities named in the SEBI circular of **2 May 2008**; **close-ended, listed** |
| REITs / InvITs | **Not MF schemes**; REIT assets **≥Rs 500 cr**, offer **≥Rs 250 cr**; InvIT offer **≥Rs 250 cr**, assets **≥Rs 500 cr**; minimum lot **Rs 10,000–15,000**, traded lot **1 unit** |
| ESG | Under **thematic** equity; 6 strategies: exclusion, integration, best-in-class, impact, sustainable objectives, transition |
| Smart beta | Index-based but **not market-cap weighted** (e.g. equal weight) |
| Quant fund | Model-driven; **removes the human element** |

#### Specialised Investment Fund (SIF)

- Fills the gap **between MF and PMS**; minimum **Rs 10 lakh** across strategies **of the same AMC**.
- **Every SIF strategy: max 25% unhedged short exposure**; only **one strategy per category**.

| Strategy | Type | Rule | Redemptions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Equity Long-Short | Equity | **≥80%** equity | **Daily or less often** |
| Equity Ex-Top 100 | Equity | **≥65%** | **Daily or less often** |
| Sector Rotation | Equity | **≥80%**, **max 4 sectors** | **Daily or less often** |
| Sectoral debt | Debt (interval) | **≥2 sectors**, **max 75%** per sector | **Weekly or less often** |
| Hybrid Long-Short | Hybrid (interval) | **≥25% each** in equity and debt | **Twice a week or less often** |

| Measure | Then | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Industry AUM | **Rs 11.89 lakh cr** (Mar 2015) | **Rs 82.03 lakh cr** (Feb 2026), **19.3% p.a.** over 11 years |
| Folios | **4.8 cr** (Mar 2010) | **27.39 cr** (Mar 2026) |
| Monthly SIP flow | **Rs 8,055 cr** (Mar 2019) | **Rs 32,087 cr** (Mar 2026) |
| SIP assets | | **Rs 15.11 lakh cr** (Mar 2026) |
| MF share of household financial investment | **10%** (2016) | **14%** (2018); bank deposits **71% → 65%** |

### Exam traps

- Unit capital = units x **face value**; AUM = units x **NAV**.
- Close-ended schemes are compulsorily listed **except ELSS**.
- Credit risk fund: 65% in **AA and below** (AA+ excluded); corporate bond fund: 80% in **AA+ and above**.
- Mid cap = **101-250**, not 101-200; small cap = **251 onwards**, by full market cap.
- Focused fund: max **30** stocks, not 20, with 80% equity.
- Large cap, dividend yield, value, contra, sectoral, thematic: **80%**; mid cap, small cap and flexi cap: **65%**.
- ELSS: **80%** equity, not 65%, with a **3-year** lock-in; 80C deduction in the **Old** Tax Regime only.
- Index funds, ETFs and FoFs: **95%**, not 80%.
- Ultra-Short-Term = **3-6 months**; Ultra Short to Short Term (old low duration) = **6-12 months**.
- Balanced Advantage = **dynamic** equity/debt; Balanced Hybrid = 40-60% equity with **no arbitrage**.

**Workbook sample answers:** 1-b (NAV), 2-c (economies of scale), 3-c (close-ended), 4-c (no limit if overlap <50%), 5-b (equity down, debt up).

## Chapter 3 – Legal Structure of Mutual Funds (4 marks)

- MF definition: a fund set up as a **trust**, **granted registration under Regulation 3**, raising money by selling **units** to the public under **schemes**, to invest in securities, money market, **gold, silver** and other assets SEBI specifies.

#### Legal basis

| Item | Governed by |
| --- | --- |
| Governing regulation | **SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026** |
| Trust | **Indian Trusts Act, 1882** |
| Trust deed registration | **Indian Registration Act, 1908** |

#### Who creates or appoints whom

| Entity | Created / appointed by | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trust | **Sponsor** creates it | **beneficiaries = unitholders** |
| AMC | **sponsor or trustees** | With **SEBI approval** |
| **Custodian** | **trustees** | |
| **RTA** | **AMC** | |
| **Collecting banker** | **AMC** | |
| **Scheme auditor** | **trustees** | Must **differ** from the **AMC auditor** |
| AMC auditor | The AMC | |

#### Key agreements

| Document | Between |
| --- | --- |
| **Trust deed** | Executed between **sponsor and trustees** |
| **Investment Management Agreement** | Trustees sign it with the AMC |

#### Sponsor

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Role | **Sponsor applies** to SEBI for MF registration and invests in the **AMC's capital** |
| Experience | **5 years** in financial services |
| Net worth | **positive net worth** in each of the last **5 years** |
| Profit | **net profit** in each of the last **5 years** |
| Average profit | **≥ Rs 10 crore** over 5 years |
| **Liquid net worth** | Must exceed the sponsor's proposed **capital contribution** to the AMC |

#### Trustees

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Form | Individuals (**Board of Trustees**) or a **trustee company** (acts through its Board of Directors) |
| Eligibility (**Regulation 11**) | **fit and proper** (Schedule II, Intermediaries Regulations 2008); **not a director, officer or employee of an AMC**; **not trustee of another MF**; furnishes information SEBI asks for |
| Appointment | Needs **prior SEBI approval** |
| Independence | At least **two-thirds** of the trustee board = **independent directors** (not associates of the sponsor, AMC or trustee company) |

#### Trustee duties (6 heads)

| # | Duty head |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | **appoint/terminate the AMC** |
| 2 | **conflicts of interest and broker empanelment** |
| 3 | **regulatory compliance** |
| 4 | **oversight for investor interests** |
| 5 | **additional oversight** |
| 6 | Due diligence on matters SEBI specifies |

#### AMC

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Directors | **professional experience** in finance; directors and key personnel must be **fit and proper** |
| Independence | At least **50%** of AMC directors must **not be associates** of the sponsor, its subsidiaries or the trustees |
| Board limits | AMC directors (**incl. Chairman**) can't sit on the board of **any MF's trustee company**; no one can be a director of **more than one AMC** |
| Director appointment | Needs **prior trustee approval** |
| Net worth | **≥ Rs 50 crore** (from **1 Jan 2024**); maintained **continuously**; **sponsor's responsibility** |
| Change of control | Needs **prior approval of trustees and SEBI** |
| On change of control | Letter to each unitholder; ad in **1 English national daily + 1 regional-language paper**; **exit at NAV with no exit load for ≥30 calendar days** |
| Removal | By **majority of trustees** or **75% of unitholders**; any change needs **SEBI + unitholder approval** |

#### Custodian

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Registration | Under **SEBI (Custodian) Regulations, 1996** |
| Independence | Can't be one where the sponsor holds **≥50% voting rights** or **≥50% of directors**, unless conditions are met |
| Role | **Custodian tracks corporate actions** (dividends, bonus, rights) and settles trades |

#### Compliance, fund management, accounting and risk

| Function | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **Compliance Officer** | Signs the **due-diligence certificate** on new scheme documents; reports to the **AMC head**; flags non-compliance **directly to trustees** |
| Fund management: **analysts** | Find ideas |
| Fund management: **fund managers** | Own scheme performance |
| Fund management: **dealers** | Place orders |
| **Fund accounting** team | Calculates NAV **daily**; separate from the AMC's **accounts** team |
| Risk officers | SEBI **Risk Management Framework**: dedicated officers for **investment, compliance, operational, cyber** risk |

#### Other entities

| Who | Key rule |
| --- | --- |
| **Fund accountant** | **No SEBI registration** needed; can be in-house |
| **RTA** | **Must register with SEBI**, but appointing one is **optional**; its offices act as **ISCs** (Investor Service Centres) |
| MFD | **NISM V-A + AMFI registration**; the limited-scheme cadre needs **NISM V-B** |
| **KRAs** | SEBI regulations in **2011**; one KYC across the securities market, under **PMLA** |
| Valuation agencies | Fair valuation of non-traded debt uses **≥2 valuation agencies**: **CRISIL and ICRA**, appointed by AMFI; AMCs use their **valuation level** |
| Depositories | **NSDL and CDSL**; investors deal through **DPs** (like bank branches) |

- **FMP**: may disclose **proposed rating profile** before investing; can then invest only **at that rating or safer**.
- **ETF units must be in demat**.

#### Transaction platforms

| Platform | Key point |
| --- | --- |
| Exchange platforms | **BSE StAR MF** and **NSE NMF-II** |
| **MF Utilities** | An **AMFI industry** platform |
| **Execution Only Platform** | Only for **direct plans**; RIA and broker platforms for their own clients are **not EOPs** |
| **EOP Category 1** | Registers with **AMFI**; body corporate; **agent of AMCs** |
| **EOP Category 2** | Registers as a **stock broker** in the exchange EOP segment; **agent of investor** |

#### AMFI

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| What it is | **AMFI** = association of all AMCs |
| Powers | Issues **ARN**; can **cancel ARN** for code violations |
| Status | **AMFI is neither a regulator nor an SRO** |

### Exam traps

- Custodian is appointed by the **trustees**, not the AMC; the RTA and collecting banker are appointed by the **AMC**.
- Trust deed is between the **sponsor and trustees**; the Investment Management Agreement is between the **trustees and AMC**.
- Sponsor eligibility uses **5 years** throughout (experience, net worth, profit), with average profit **≥ Rs 10 crore**.
- Trustee board: **two-thirds** independent; AMC board: **50%** non-associate.
- AMC net worth **≥ Rs 50 crore**, maintained continuously, is the **sponsor's** responsibility.
- AMC removal: **majority of trustees** or **75% of unitholders**; any change needs SEBI + unitholder approval.
- Fund accountant needs **no SEBI registration**; the RTA **must register** with SEBI but appointing one is **optional**.
- **AMFI** is neither a regulator nor an SRO; CRISIL and ICRA are appointed by **AMFI**, not SEBI.

**Workbook sample answers:** 1-c (unitholders), 2-b (custodian), 3-a (association of AMCs), 4-c (CRISIL and ICRA), 5-a (AMC).

## Chapter 4 – Legal and Regulatory Framework (10 marks)

### Regulators and SEBI

#### The four regulators

| Regulator | Regulates |
| --- | --- |
| RBI | Banks, money market |
| SEBI | Securities |
| IRDAI | Insurance |
| PFRDA | Pension |

- All 4 are under the **Ministry of Finance**.
- RBI rules also apply to MFs for **money market, overseas investment, forex**.

#### SEBI and MF regulation

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Who regulates MFs | **SEBI regulates mutual funds**; **AMFI does not** |
| MF regulations | First issued in **1996**, now replaced by the **MF Regulations 2026** |
| "Securities" | Transferable, tradeable instruments, defined in **Sec 2(h) of the SCRA, 1956** |
| SEBI preamble | **Protect investors, develop and regulate** the securities market |

#### Benchmarks, risk class and crisis reforms

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Benchmark | **Total Return Index (TRI)**, which includes dividends and interest; not a **Price Return Index** |

| Segregated portfolio | Separates distressed assets after a **credit event**; introduced **after** the **2018** credit crisis |
| **2008** crisis reforms | **No premature redemption in FMPs**; **"liquid plus"** name banned |

### Investment restrictions

#### What an MF can invest in

| Rule | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| **Regulation 39** | MF may invest **only** in securities, money market instruments, **privately placed debentures**, **securitised debt** (asset or mortgage backed), **gold**, **silver** and anything else SEBI specifies |
| **Sixth Schedule** | Equity and debt only if **listed or to be listed**, except **unlisted G-secs and money market instruments (not CPs)** and **unlisted NCDs** as SEBI allows; **unrated debt** on SEBI's conditions |
| **SIF** (**Regulation 51**) | Same permitted instruments as MFs (Reg 39); the **Sixth Schedule** restrictions apply |

#### Regulation 41

| Area | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| All investments | Follow the **Sixth Schedule** (except **gold and silver ETFs**) |
| Settlement | **Only in demat** |
| Carry-forward | **No carry-forward** deals except as SEBI allows |
| Derivatives and **short selling** | Only on a **recognised stock exchange** |
| **Inter-scheme transfers** | Only on SEBI's conditions |

#### Other investment rules

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| **Gold/silver ETFs** | Invest **only in gold/silver** (or related instruments), except for **redemption liquidity**; may use **exchange-traded commodity derivatives**; idle money in **short-term bank deposits** |
| Scheme into **another MF scheme** | Allowed with **no fees** (not for FoFs) |
| Investing in a FoF | **No scheme can invest in a FoF** |
| FoF | **Can't invest in another FoF**; holds **only MF units** (plus liquidity) |
| Trades | **Delivery basis** only (short selling and derivatives allowed under SEBI's framework) |
| Holding | Securities held **in the MF's name** for the scheme |
| Money awaiting deployment | Can sit in **short-term deposits of scheduled commercial banks** |
| Limits | All investments follow SEBI's **prudential limits** |

## Chapter 7 – NAV, TER and Pricing of Units (8 marks)

### Valuation

#### Valuation framework

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Valuation norms | **Seventh Schedule** (MF Regulations 2026) |
| Goal | **Fair treatment** of entering, exiting and staying investors |
| Principles | **10 fair valuation principles**; if they **conflict with the Valuation Guidelines**, the **Principles prevail** |
| Principle 1 | Value = **realisable value**, in **good faith** |
| Policy approval | **AMC Board**; a **new security type** needs its valuation method set **first** |
| Policy review | By an **independent auditor at least once a year** |
| Policy disclosure | In the **SAI** and on the website |
| Responsibility | The **AMC**, for **true and fair** valuation |
| Deviation | AMC may **deviate** from policy (report to both **Boards**, disclose to investors) |
| Audit trail | Valuation rationale kept for **audit trail** (**Seventh Schedule**) |

#### Valuation by security type

| Security | How valued |
| --- | --- |
| Debt | Trade prices from **all public platforms** |
| Traded equity | **Last closing price** on the **principal exchange** (reasons in **writing**) |
| Equity not traded that day | Use **another exchange**, or the **earliest previous day within 30 days** |
| **No trade for 30 days = non-traded** | Valued **in good faith** by AMC Board methods; reviewed by trustees; auditors report them **"fair and reasonable"** |
| Non-traded equity | **Capitalisation of earnings** (P/E of comparables) ± book value, **less an illiquidity discount** |
| Convertibles | Value the debt and equity parts **separately** |
| Warrants | **Share value − exercise price − discount** |
| **Gold ETF** | **LBMA AM fixing**, USD per troy ounce, **995.0** fineness |
| **Silver ETF** | LBMA AM fixing, **999.0** fineness |

### NAV

#### NAV formula

| Measure | Formula | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NAV | **Net assets ÷ units outstanding** = **(total assets − liabilities other than to unitholders) ÷ units** | Workbook: **Rs 217 cr ÷ 20 cr = Rs 10.85** |

#### NAV rules

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Net assets | Include **unrealised gains** |
| **Accrual principle** | Income and expenses count when **due**, not when paid |
| Short-term borrowing for liquidity | **≤ 6 months** |
| NAV rises with | More **income and gains** |
| NAV falls with | More **expenses** |

- **Mark to market** stops dilution:

#### Mark to market and dilution

| Action | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Issuing units | **below NAV lowers** NAV for existing investors |
| Redeeming | **below NAV raises** it |

### Exam traps

- If the fair valuation principles conflict with the Valuation Guidelines, the **principles** prevail, not the guidelines.
- The valuation policy is approved by the **AMC Board**, not the trustees, and reviewed by an independent auditor at least **once a year**.
- No trade for **30 days** = non-traded; non-traded equity is valued with an illiquidity **discount**, not a premium.
- Gold ETF: LBMA **AM** fixing at **995.0** fineness (USD per troy ounce); Silver ETF: LBMA AM fixing at **999.0**.
- NAV deducts liabilities **other than to unitholders**; net assets **include** unrealised gains.
- Issuing units below NAV **lowers** NAV for existing investors; redeeming below NAV **raises** it.
- Distributable surplus: valuation **gains ignored**, valuation **losses deducted**.
- **Trustees** decide the IDCW amount and record date, not the AMC; notice within **1 day**, record date **5 calendar days** from notice.
- Close-ended / interval TER: equity **1.00%**, others **0.80%**; index fund / ETF **0.90%**.
- AT1 deemed maturity = **100 years** from issue; AT2 = **contractual** maturity; AT1 valued on yield to **call**, not maturity.

## Chapter 8 – Taxation (4 marks)

- Tax depends on: **type of income**, **holding period**, **type of scheme**, **type of investor**, **purchase date** (**1 Apr 2023**) and **redemption date** (**23 Jul 2024**).

#### Basics

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| MF scheme income | **tax exempt** under **Sec 10(23D)**, because MFs are **trusts** |
| **Growth** option | **capital gains only** |
| **IDCW** option | Dividend **+** capital gains |
| **Equity-oriented** for tax | **>65%** in **listed Indian equity** |
| A **FoF investing in equity MFs** | **non-equity** for tax |
| Joint holding | Income taxed in the **first holder's** hands |

#### Holding periods

| Long-term after | Applies to |
| --- | --- |
| **12 months** | **listed securities and equity funds** |
| **24 months** | Others |

- Holding periods: only **12 or 24 months**.

#### Grandfathering

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Rule | Gains up to **31 Jan 2018** exempt; cost = **higher of actual cost or 31 Jan 2018 NAV** |
| Example | Cost Rs 1 lakh, 31 Jan 2018 value Rs 2 lakh |
| Sold at **Rs 1.8 lakh** | **zero gain** |
| Sold at **Rs 2.2 lakh** | **Rs 20,000** gain |

#### Equity LTCG exemption

| LTCG in the year | Tax |
| --- | --- |
| Exempt limit | Up to **Rs 1.25 lakh** a year |
| Rs 90,000 | **no tax** |
| Rs 1.35 lakh | Tax on **Rs 10,000** |

#### Capital gains tax by scheme type

| Scheme type | Short-term gains | Long-term gains | Long-term after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Equity-oriented (incl. hybrids **>65% equity**) | **20%** | **12.5%**; **Rs 1.25 lakh** exempt per FY | **12 months** |
| Debt-oriented (incl. hybrids **>65% debt**) | **Slab rate** | **Slab rate** | — |
| Hybrid with **35–65%** domestic equity | **Slab rate** | **12.5%** | **24 months** |
| Other MFs (FoFs, international funds/FoFs) | **Slab rate** | **12.5%** | **24 months** |
| Equity ETFs and Gold ETFs | **Slab rate** | **12.5%** | **12 months** (as printed) |
| Debt ETFs | **Slab rate** | **Slab rate** | — |

#### IDCW taxation

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| DDT | **DDT abolished** by Budget **Feb 2020** |
| Since **April 2020** | IDCW taxed at the investor's **slab rate**; tax-exempt investors pay **nil** |
| Effect on NAV | IDCW **reduces NAV by more** than the investor's post-tax receipt |
| Growth vs IDCW | **Growth option is more tax-efficient** because of **tax deferral** |
| From **1 Apr 2021** | IDCW is split into **income distribution** and **capital distribution** for tax |

#### Stamp duty

| Transaction | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| **Issue** (purchase, SIP, STP, switch-in, IDCW reinvestment) | **0.005%** |
| **Transfer** | **0.015%** |

- **Stamp duty** applies from **1 Jul 2020**; units are allotted on the amount **after stamp duty**.

#### Capital losses

| Loss | Set off against |
| --- | --- |
| **STCL** | **STCG or LTCG** |
| **LTCL** | Only **LTCG** |
| Any capital loss | **Can't be set off against salary** or other income heads |
| Unabsorbed capital loss | **Carried forward up to 8 years** |

- **Bonus stripping**: loss **disallowed** if bought **≤3 months before** the record date and sold **≤9 months after**; the loss becomes the **cost of the bonus units**. Even outside this window, the practice is **discouraged**.

#### Securities Transaction Tax (STT)

| Transaction | STT |
| --- | --- |
| Purchase | **None on purchase** |
| Debt funds | **None on debt funds** |
| Redemption to the MF | **0.001%** |
| Delivery sale on exchange | **0.001%** |
| Non-delivery | **0.025%** |

- **STT** applies only to **equity-oriented funds**, on **sale or redemption**; all paid by the **seller**.

#### ELSS and Sec 80C

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| **ELSS (Tax Saver)** | 80C up to **Rs 1.5 lakh** (individual/**HUF**), **Old Tax Regime only**, **3-year lock-in** |
| Lock-in start | Runs from **each SIP instalment** and **each IDCW reinvestment** |
| Who gets the tax benefit | The **first holder** |
| Under the **new tax regime** | ELSS is just an **equity fund with a lock-in** |
| Some **old retirement funds** | Also give **80C**, with a **5-year lock-in** |

#### TDS

| Investor and income | TDS |
| --- | --- |
| **Residents**, redemption | **No TDS** |
| Residents, IDCW | **10%** if dividends exceed **Rs 10,000** a year |
| NRIs | **lower of the Income Tax Act rate or the DTAA rate** (with documents) |

#### GST and TER

| GST on | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| **Management fee** | Charged **over and above** TER |
| **Other fees** | **Within** TER |
| **Brokerage/transaction cost** | **Within** TER |
| **Exit load** | Deducted **from the load** before crediting the scheme |
| **Distributor commission** | Can't be charged to the scheme |

- From **1 April 2026**: cost = **base expense ratio + brokerage (within limits) + statutory levies (e.g. GST)**. BER = advisory fees + other expenses + brokerage commission, **excluding statutory levies** and transaction cost; nothing else may be charged except **exit load**.

### Exam traps

- A FoF investing in equity MFs is **non-equity** for tax, not equity-oriented; equity-oriented = **more than 65%** in **listed Indian** equity.
- Hybrids with 35-65% domestic equity and other MFs (FoFs, international) turn long-term after **24 months**, not 12; equity funds after **12 months**.
- Grandfathering cut-off is **31 Jan 2018**; cost = **higher** of actual cost or 31 Jan 2018 NAV, not lower.
- Stamp duty: **0.005%** on issue, **0.015%** on transfer, from **1 Jul 2020**; units are allotted on the amount **after** stamp duty.
- STCL sets off against **STCG or LTCG**; LTCL **only against LTCG**; neither against salary; carry forward **8 years**.
- STT applies only on **sale/redemption** of **equity-oriented** funds and is paid by the **seller**; none on purchase, none on debt funds.
- NRI TDS is at the **lower** of the IT Act rate or DTAA rate, not the higher; residents have **no TDS** on redemption.
- GST on **management fee** is **over and above** TER; GST on other fees and brokerage is **within** TER; GST on distributor commission can't be charged to the scheme.

### Exam traps

- Late NFO refund interest at **15% p.a.** is paid by the **AMC**, not the scheme.
- NFO stays open **min 3 working days, max 15 days**; **ELSS** is exempt from the maximum, not the minimum.
- Only **open-ended** NFOs have a re-opening date (within **5 business days of allotment**); close-ended have none.

- Time stamp is at the **OPoA**, not when the distributor receives the form.
- Nominee holds units **in trust for legal heirs**; a surviving joint holder takes them as **owner**, and ranks **above** the nominee.

## Chapter 10 – Risk, Return and Performance of Funds (7 marks)

### Risk factors

- The SID lists **standard (general)** and **specific** risk factors, plus **mitigation**.
- Standard statements: past performance **no guarantee**; scheme **name says nothing about quality**; sponsor not liable beyond its **initial contribution**.
- General risks: liquidity, interest rate, **reinvestment** ("interest on interest"), political, economic, **foreign currency** (for FPIs; the **AMC doesn't manage** it), settlement/counterparty, exchange-transaction.

#### Risks by instrument or strategy

| Instrument / strategy | Key risk |
| --- | --- |
| **Money market** securities | Liquid but **no well-developed secondary market** |
| **Securities lending** | Borrower returns equivalent securities plus corporate benefits; risk = counterparty failure |
| **Short-selling** | Counterparty and **illiquidity** of the borrowed stock |
| Equity by market cap | Moving down from **large to mid to small caps** increases volatility and liquidity risk |
| Derivatives | **Leveraged**; **counterparty** (the exchange guarantees settlement), **market liquidity**, **model** (mispricing), **basis** (hedge vs underlying) |
| Debt | Reinvestment, **rating migration** (AAA → AA+ = price falls), term structure, credit |
| Floaters | **Spread risk** and **basis risk** |
| Repo in corporate bonds | Counterparty risk, mitigated by **over-collateralisation** |
| Segregated portfolio | May be **illiquid**, may realise **nothing**, listing **doesn't ensure** liquidity |

#### Securitisation

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Structure | **Securitisation**: originator sells receivables to an **SPV (trust)**, which issues **PTCs** |
| Pool checks | **Lower LTV = safer**; **seasoning** (more instalments already paid = better); **DPD** buckets |
| **Credit enhancement** | Lets PTCs be rated **above the originator**; they are **non-recourse** |
| Risks | **Prepayment**, originator bankruptcy (**true sale**), **co-mingling** |
| What MFs buy | Mostly securitised paper rated **AA and above** |

#### Systematic vs unsystematic risk

| | Systematic | Unsystematic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source | Market | Company-specific |
| Diversifiable? | **Non-diversifiable** | **Diversifiable** |
| Example | Inflation | A strike |

- Investors are **rewarded only for systematic risk**.
- The fund manager can cut systematic risk only by **holding cash** (limited by SEBI).

### Drivers of return

- Equity: **security selection** and **market timing**.

#### Fundamental vs technical analysis

| Analysis | Suits |
| --- | --- |
| **Fundamental analysis** | The **long term** |
| **Technical analysis** (price-volume "**chartists**") | The **short term and timing** |

#### Valuation ratios

| Measure | Formula / reading |
| --- | --- |
| **EPS** | PAT ÷ shares |
| **P/E** | Price ÷ EPS; **forward P/E** uses projected EPS |
| PEG | **PEG = 1** fair; **<1** undervalued; **>1** overvalued |
| **Book value/share** | Net worth ÷ shares |
| **P/BV** | Price ÷ BV |
| **Dividend yield** | DPS ÷ price; it **falls in bull markets**, **rises in bear markets** |

#### Growth vs value stocks

| Style | Features |
| --- | --- |
| **Growth** | **High P/E and PEG, low dividend yield**; fall more in corrections |
| **Value** | Priced **below intrinsic value**; need a **long horizon** |

- High **valuation** ≠ high **price**.
- **EIC** framework: **Economy, Industry, Company**.

#### Top-down vs bottom-up

| Approach | Starts with | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Top-down** | Economy first | Avoids a bad **sector** |
| **Bottom-up** | Stock first | Sector allocation is the **result** |

- Debt returns are driven by **interest rates and credit spreads**.

#### Debt instruments and issuers

| Instrument | Issuer / tenor |
| --- | --- |
| **T-bills** | Issued by **RBI** for the GoI |
| **CDs** | Banks **7 days–1 year**, FIs **1–3 years** |
| **CP** | Companies, **up to 1 year** |
| **Bonds** | Government issues them |
| **Debentures** | Private companies issue them |

#### Debt return terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Credit spread** | Corporate yield − **gilt** yield |
| Rating agencies | **CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, India Ratings (Fitch)** |
| **YTM** | Return if **held to maturity** |
| **HPR** | Interest + capital gain or loss |
| Floater coupon | **Base + spread**; it **holds its value** when rates move |
| Premium example | A 9.5% AAA bond when market yield is 8.5% trades at a **premium** |

#### Rate view and positioning

| Rate view | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Rates **expected up** | Shorten duration or buy floaters |
| Rates **expected down** | Buy **long** duration |

#### What drives fund returns

| Fund / asset | Return driver |
| --- | --- |
| Debt funds | Hinge on **interest-rate calls** |
| Equity funds | **Sector and stock** calls |
| Liquid, ultra-short and floater funds | Focus on **accrual** |
| **Gold** | **Global price + rupee** (**weaker rupee = higher** return) + duties; gold funds are **passive** |
| **Real estate** | A **local** asset; driven by economy, infrastructure, rates; **no MF real estate schemes** currently; **REITs** are gaining popularity with SEBI support |

### Returns

#### Simple and annualised return

| Measure | Rule and examples |
| --- | --- |
| **Simple** return | 12 → 15 = **25%**; 120 → 135 = **12.5%** |
| **Annualised** | Simple × 12 ÷ months: 5% in 6 months = **10%**; 3% in 4 months = **9%** |

<p class="formula">CAGR = (Later value ÷ Initial value)<sup>1/n</sup> − 1</p>

#### CAGR examples

| Case | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Rs 1,000 to Rs 4,000 in 2 yrs | **100%** CAGR |
| Rs 10,000 at 10% for 3 yrs | **Rs 13,310** (vs **13,000** simple) |
| n | **Days ÷ 365** |

- With dividends, SEBI uses the **reinvestment method** (reinvest at **ex-dividend NAV**): workbook example **1,000 → 1,080 → 1,152 units**, value **Rs 17,280**, **43.65%** over **1.51 years**.
- **Investor return ≠ scheme return** because of **loads**, **taxes** and **cash-flow timing**; advertised returns **ignore loads**.

#### Which return to quote

| Holding period | Use |
| --- | --- |
| **<1 year** | **Absolute** |
| **>1 year** | **CAGR** |

- **Rolling returns** = average of many consecutive periods; removes **start/end-date bias**.
- **No promised returns** except **assured-return schemes**, which need a named **guarantor** in the SID; so far **no assured-return product** has been launched, only **capital protection-oriented** funds.

### Risk from the investor's view

#### Investor-view risk lessons

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| "**Low risk ≠ zero risk**" | Liquid and ultra-short funds faced **credit events** in **2018 and 2019** |
| Without segregation | Redemptions **raise** the bad paper's share: 5% becomes **10%** after 50% redemptions |
| Arbitrage funds | Those using **paired arbitrage, alpha hedging, merger arbitrage** take views, so they're **not risk-free** |
| Schemes being **wound up** | Are **listed** (**Clause 37(d)** of the MF Regulations) |
| Real estate funds | **High risk** but **less risky than direct property** |

### Risk measures

#### Risk measures

| Measure | What it shows | Notes / example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Variance** | Fluctuation vs **own average** | Workbook example **0.67 vs 39.58** |
| **SD** | **√variance** = **total risk** | For **debt and equity**; needs **≥30 observations** |
| **Beta** (from **CAPM**) | **Systematic** risk vs a diversified index (**β = 1**) | **Equity only**; beta 0.8 moves **8%** when the market moves 10%; beta 1.2 moves **12%** |
| **Modified duration** | Interest rate sensitivity | The **professional's** measure |
| **WAM** | Simpler; used with **lay investors** | 70% × 4 yrs + 30% × 1 yr = **3.1 years** |
| **Credit rating** profile | Default risk | **G-secs and cash have no credit risk** |

#### Annualising SD

| Data frequency | Multiply by |
| --- | --- |
| Weekly | **×√52** (0.65 → **4.70%**) |
| Monthly | **×√12** |
| Daily | **×√252** |

### Credit events, gating and side-pocketing

- **Credit event** = **default, delay or downgrade**.
- Rs 10,000 cr scheme with 8% in one paper; 20% redemptions push exposure to **10%**.

#### Gating

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| What | **Gating** = restricting redemptions |
| When | Only in **market-wide** crises (liquidity, **exchange closure**, operational **force majeure**); **never for one bad security** |
| Limit | **Max 10 working days in any 90 days** |
| Approval | **AMC Board + trustee** approval; **SEBI informed immediately** |
| During gating | The **first Rs 2 lakh** of every redemption is paid normally |

#### Segregated portfolio (side-pocketing)

| Point | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Date / status | **Segregated portfolio** (Dec **2018**): **optional** |
| Trigger | A **downgrade**; unrated debt can be segregated **only on actual default**, after AMFI informs all AMCs (**7 Nov 2019**) |
| New schemes after **7 Nov 2019** | Must carry **enabling provisions** in the SID |
| Policy | Approved by **trustees**; effective from the **credit-event day**; **press release** issued |
| Investors | Get **equal units** in the segregated portfolio; **no subscription or redemption** in it; **listed within 10 working days** |
| New investors | Get **main-portfolio units only** |
| Costs | **No management fee**; TER only **on recovery**, capped at the **main portfolio's average**; legal costs within TER, **excess borne by the AMC**; **never charged to the main portfolio** |
| Workbook example | Total NAV **1,000.1227** = main **876.2427** + segregated **123.88** |

- Mitigants: **longer holding** (**10 yrs = almost no negative returns** historically); **match holding period to portfolio maturity**.

### Exam traps

- **SD** = total risk (debt and equity); **beta** = systematic risk only (equity only).
- **Systematic** risk is non-diversifiable (e.g. inflation); **unsystematic** is diversifiable (e.g. a strike); investors are rewarded only for **systematic** risk.
- Annualise SD with **√52** weekly, **√12** monthly, **√252** daily, not 52, 12 or 252.
- **Technical** analysis suits the short term and timing; **fundamental** analysis suits the long term.
- Dividend yield **falls** in bull markets and **rises** in bear markets.
- Rates expected up: **shorten** duration or buy floaters; rates expected down: buy **long** duration.
- Credit spread = corporate yield − **gilt** yield, not coupon − YTM.
- CP: up to **1 year**; CDs: banks **7 days–1 year**, FIs **1–3 years**.
- Gating only in **market-wide** crises, max **10 working days in any 90 days**; never for one bad security.
- Weaker rupee = **higher** gold fund return; lower LTV = **safer** securitised pool.

**Workbook sample answers:** 1-a (True), 2-c (technical), 3-c (12.5%), 4-a (variance).

### Performance measures

- **Relative return** vs benchmark or peers **ignores risk**.
- **AMCs and trustees** review relative returns **periodically**.

<p class="formula">Sharpe = (R<sub>s</sub> − R<sub>f</sub>) ÷ SD &nbsp;·&nbsp; Treynor = (R<sub>s</sub> − R<sub>f</sub>) ÷ Beta &nbsp;·&nbsp; IR = (R<sub>p</sub> − R<sub>b</sub>) ÷ SD of excess return</p>

- R<sub>s</sub> − R<sub>f</sub> = **risk premium**; the risk-free rate = **T-bill** return.

#### Risk-adjusted measures

| Measure | Formula / basis | Example / use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sharpe** | Uses **SD** (total risk) | (7 − 5) ÷ 0.5 = **4**; higher = better; compare **like with like** (never equity vs debt) |
| **Treynor** | Uses **beta** (systematic risk) | (8 − 5) ÷ 1.2 = **2.5**; for **diversified equity** |
| **Alpha** | Actual − **beta-expected (optimal)** return | **Positive = outperformance**; for diversified equity |
| **Information Ratio** | **Excess return over the Tier-1 benchmark ÷ SD of excess return** (daily data) | The **only mandatory** risk-adjusted measure; disclosed **daily** on the AMC and AMFI sites (**Jan 2025** circular) |
| **Tracking error** | **SD of excess returns** | Index fund: how closely it tracks its index (**ideally zero**); active fund: how **consistently** it beats its benchmark (**lower = more consistent**) |

- Risk-adjusted **"calculations are arithmetic; evaluation is an art"**.

### Disclosure sources

#### Where performance is disclosed

| Source | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **SID** | Updated (with performance) **twice a year** |
| **Factsheet** | **Monthly** but **not mandatory**; ad rules apply to it |
| Product label | Shows the **objective** (wealth creation / income / liquidity), **asset class**, **holding period** and **risk-o-meter** |
| **AMFI website** | Performance data for **all schemes** |
| Data vendors | Include **Morningstar and Value Research** |

- **Discrete annual returns** show **consistency** across market conditions.

#### Factsheet macro readings

| Signal | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| High **fiscal deficit** | Pushes **interest rates up** |
| **Inflation** above comfort | Brings **monetary tightening** |
| Low **global rates** | Pull **flows into emerging markets** |
| **Oil** prices | Move the **currency** |

### Exam traps

- **Sharpe** divides by **SD** (total risk); **Treynor** divides by **beta** (systematic risk); both share the numerator (scheme return − T-bill return).
- Information Ratio = excess return over the **Tier-1 benchmark** ÷ SD of that excess return, not (scheme return − T-bill return).
- **Tier 1 = category**, **Tier 2 = fund manager's style**, not the other way round.
- TRI since **1 Feb 2018**; two-tier benchmarks effective **1 Jan 2022** (circular Oct 2021); debt Tier-1 on PRC from **1 Apr 2022**.
- **Si-Bex 1–3 yrs**, **Mi-Bex 3–7 yrs**, **Li-Bex >7 yrs**.
- IR is the **only mandatory** risk-adjusted measure, disclosed **daily** on AMC and AMFI sites, not Sharpe and not monthly.
- Factsheet is **monthly but not mandatory**; the SID (with performance) is updated **twice a year**.
- Single-fund FoF uses the **underlying's** benchmark; multi-fund FoF uses a **broad market index**.
- **Lower** tracking error = more consistent outperformance; an index fund's tracking error is ideally **zero**.
- Benchmark is chosen by the **AMC with the trustees**, never on **past returns**; a change must be justified and documented.

## Chapter 12 – Mutual Fund Scheme Selection (15 marks)

### Investor fit

#### Steps

| Step | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Step 1 | **Set goals** |
| Step 2 | **Assess situation and risk appetite** (**need + ability + willingness**) |

#### Needs map to funds

| Need | Fund |
| --- | --- |
| **Growth** | **Equity** |
| **Income** | **Debt** |
| **Parking** | **Liquid** |

- Equity needs a **long horizon**, even for an investor willing to take risk.
- **Age alone is a poor guide**; use the **time horizon to each goal**.

#### Core vs satellite

| Portfolio | Purpose | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Core** | Long-term goals | Diversified, large, mid cap |
| **Satellite** | Tactical | **Sector funds**, **long gilt** (rates expected down), **gold** (high inflation or uncertainty) |

- Workbook illustration: a moderate investor at **80% core / 20% satellite**.

### Risk hierarchies (left to right)

#### Risk orderings

| Dimension | Order (left to right) |
| --- | --- |
| Overall: **risk and return rise** | Liquid → debt → hybrid → equity |
| **Interest rate risk rises** | Overnight → liquid → ultra-short → low → short → medium → medium-long → long |
| **Credit risk rises** | **Gilt** → banking & PSU → corporate bond → **credit risk fund** |
| **Equity risk falls** | Small → mid → multi → large & mid → **large** (lowest) |
| **Concentration risk rises** | Diversified → **focused** → thematic → **sector** |
| **Hybrid risk rises** | **Arbitrage** → **conservative** → equity savings → dynamic asset allocation → multi-asset → balanced → **aggressive** |

- **Product labelling** (2013) added a disclaimer to **consult a financial adviser** if unsure of suitability; the **risk-o-meter** is covered in Ch 5.

### Choosing by strategy

#### Passive vs active

| Type | Key points |
| --- | --- |
| **Passive** | **No selection risk**, lower cost, but **NAV still falls** when the index falls |
| **Active** | Higher cost and risk; must **beat the benchmark** to justify the choice |

#### Open-ended vs close-ended

| Structure | Key points |
| --- | --- |
| Open-ended | Liquidity at **NAV less load**, but must hold some **cash** (dilutes returns) and faces **flow pressure** |
| Close-ended | Exit only via the exchange, often at a **discount with no limit**; price **converges to NAV near maturity**; can hold **illiquid or long-gestation** stocks; choose only if the money can stay **locked in** |

#### Equity fund choices

| Fund | When / risk |
| --- | --- |
| **Index or value** fund | Low-risk equity |
| **Growth** fund | Extra risk for extra return; outperforms in **bull** markets |
| **Value** | Outperforms in **falling** markets **or over a long period**; needs a **long horizon** |
| Focused | **Riskier than diversified** but **less risky than sector** funds |
| **Sector** | Depend on **timing**, so belong in the **satellite** portfolio |
| **Thematic** (MNC, PSU, infra) | Sit **between diversified and sector** |
| Mid/small caps | Shine in **recoveries**, when large caps turn expensive, but many **fail in downturns** |
| **International funds** | Foreign equity + **currency**: a **stronger dollar helps** an Indian investor; suit the **satellite** portfolio |

- "**Diversification is protection against ignorance.**"

#### Debt fund choices

### Choosing within a category

#### Selection checks

| Check | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| True to label | Portfolio must be **true to label** (e.g. a "value" fund full of expensive front-line stocks isn't) |
| **Performance** | **Consistent** outperformance, **downside protection**, vs **benchmark and peers**, over **rolling** periods |
| **Fund age** | Matters most where scheme returns **diverge** (e.g. **multi cap** more than large cap) |
| **Fund size** | Large helps **large-cap** funds; hurts **mid-cap or sector** funds with limited stocks |
| **Costs** | Matter most in **debt** and **index** funds |
| Star ratings | = **Past performance**, **no predictive** power; this quarter's best fund is rarely next quarter's |
| Target | The **top few** consistently: **top 3–5** in small categories, **top 10–15%** in big ones |

#### Portfolio turnover

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Formula | Purchases **or** sales (**whichever is lower**) ÷ average net assets |

### Exam traps

- Equity risk is **lowest in large cap**, then large & mid, multi, mid, and **highest in small cap**; large & mid is less risky than multi cap, not more.
- Credit risk rises **gilt → banking & PSU → corporate bond → credit risk fund**; banking & PSU is below corporate bond, not above.
- Concentration risk: diversified < focused < **thematic** < **sector**; thematic sits between diversified and sector, not above sector.
- Hybrid risk starts at **arbitrage** (lowest), not conservative, and ends at **aggressive**, which is above balanced.
- Long gilt is a satellite bet when rates are expected to **fall**; short duration (1-3 yrs) is used when rates are expected to **rise**.
- Liquid funds are for parking **up to 91 days**, not longer; holding them longer wastes return.
- **Growth** outperforms in **bull** markets; **value** outperforms in **falling** markets or over a long period, not in bull runs.
- A **stronger** dollar helps an Indian investor in an international fund, not a weaker one; international funds are **satellite**, not core.
- Turnover numerator = **lower** of purchases or sales, not the higher; 200% turnover = about **6 months** holding (too short for value, fine for momentum).
- Look-back: equity **at least 5 yrs**, long-term debt **at least 3 yrs**; target top **3-5** in small categories, top **10-15%** in big ones, not the reverse.
