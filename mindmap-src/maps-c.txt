@ch9|9|15|Investor Services|NFOs, plans and options, who can invest, payments, cut-off times, KYC, SIP/SWP/STP, nomination, transmission and service timelines.
Investor Services
  NFO and plans
    Idea from CIO (objective) and CMO (market interest)
    SID approved by trustees + AMC Board, filed with SEBI
    NFO open min 3 working days, max 15 days (ELSS exempt)
    Allot or refund in 5 business days; late = 15% p.a., paid by AMC
    Open-ended re-opens within 5 business days of allotment
    NFO price Rs 10: units = amount ÷ 10
    Demat credit 2 working days; SoA 5 business days
    ASBA: money only blocked, debited on allotment
    Trustees can reject any application without reason
    Direct vs Regular
      Direct: lower TER, no commission, separate NAV
      Write "Direct" in the ARN box
      Only these two plans can differ by expense
    SIF: Rs 10 lakh minimum at PAN level
  Options and bonus
    IDCW payout
      Cash to bank, units unchanged
      NAV falls to ex-dividend NAV
      Cum-dividend NAV: between announcement and payout
    IDCW reinvestment
      Units at ex-dividend NAV: Rs 2 × 100 units at NAV 20 = 10 units
      Still taxed at slab in the year
    Growth: no dividend, NAV holds all gains
    Workbook: NAV 10 → 12, Re 1 dividend, reinvest at 11 = 9.09 units
    Bonus 1:3: 1 free per 3 held; NAV falls, value unchanged
  Investors and the form
    Individuals: up to 3 joint holders
    Minors: guardian (natural or court-appointed); no joint holders
    HUF via the Karta
    NRI / PIO / OCI: repatriable or non-repatriable
    QFI: FATF member + IOSCO MMOU signatory
    FPI: direct (demat) or indirect (UCR)
    First holder gets all benefits
    PoA holder can do all except nominate
    Joint holders can't be added; deleted only on death
    KYC address overrides the form
    First holder's bank details mandatory
    Demat applicants: details match DP; payout to demat bank account
    Nomination: opt in or opt out; optional for joint folios
  Payments
    Modes
      NACH (NPCI) replaced ECS
      UPI via VPA; AEPS via Aadhaar + fingerprint; USSD *99#
      OTM: folio level, on NACH, 3–4 weeks to register
      Credit cards not allowed
    Limits
      E-wallet + cash: Rs 50,000 per investor per MF per FY
      Cash only for resident individuals, sole proprietors, minors
    Cheques: not post-dated (except SIP), not stale (>3 months), local / at-par
    NRI: repatriable from NRE / FCNR; non-repatriable also NRO
    Third-party: only 3 exceptions
      Parent / relative for a minor, up to Rs 50,000
      Employer payroll
      Custodian for FPI
    Bank accounts: 5 individuals, 10 non-individuals; one default
    Instant Access: liquid / overnight; lower of Rs 50,000 or 90% per day
    Redemption by amount or units, FIFO; demat via DP
    Two-factor authentication mandatory; broker pool accounts gone
  Cut-off times
    Equity and debt purchase: 3 pm, NAV of day funds available
    Liquid / overnight purchase: 1:30 pm, NAV of day before receipt
    Equity / debt redemption: 3 pm, same day; after = next day
    Liquid redemption: 3 pm, NAV of day before next business day
    Overnight redemption: 3 pm; online 7 pm
    Not for NFOs and international schemes
    Time stamp at OPoA; tamper-proof machines; web server time online
  KYC
    Everyone: joint holders, NRIs, PoA holder, guardian (not minor)
    PAN exempt
      Govt and court officials, Sikkim residents, UN bodies
      Micro-SIP + lump sum ≤ Rs 50,000 a year (PEKRN)
    KRA: one KYC across market; IPV mandatory
    CERSAI: Central KYC Records Registry; KYC Identifier
    Aadhaar e-KYC via KUA / sub-KUA
    Online: eSign, DigiLocker, OTP, penny drop
    UBO
      Company >25%
      Partnership >15%
      Trust ≥15% + settlor + trustees
      Not for listed companies
    FATCA / CRS change within 30 days
  Systematic transactions
    SIP = rupee cost averaging; average cost below average NAV
    SWP: 1% load at NAV 10 = 101.0101 units for Rs 1,000
    STP = SWP from source + SIP into target
      Scheme minimums don't apply; loads and tax do
      0.25% load on Rs 1 lakh = Rs 99,750
    DTP: needs IDCW option; invested next business day after record date
    SIP registration 15–30 days
    Top-up: +Rs 500 = 3,000 then 3,500; +10% = 2,750 then 3,025
    Minor's SIP only till majority
    Triggers are one-time: stop-loss, appreciation, date, market level
  Non-financial transactions
    Nomination
      Up to 10 nominees; % totals 100 or equal split
      Only individuals; not minor's folio or PoA holder
      Nominee: individual (even minor / NRI), govt body, religious / charitable trust
      Not: other trusts, society, company, firm, Karta
      Nominee holds in trust for legal heirs
      Demat follows demat nomination
    Pledge: all sign, out of lock-in, pledgee's NOC to sell
    Changes
      Name, address, PAN, contact: via KRA
      Bank, holding mode, nomination: each MF
      Guardian change: new KYC + old guardian's NOC
    Transmission
      Joint holder dies: survivor by name deletion, as owner
      Single holder: nominee; no nominee: legal heirs
      Needs KYC, death certificate, indemnity
    Minor turns major
      Folio frozen; SIP / SWP / STP stop
      MAM form: PAN, KYC, bank proof, attested signature
      New demat account needed
    NRI returns: resident account (RFC), new demat, update KRA
    Change of Karta: indemnity from all co-parceners
  Statements and service levels
    SoA each month with a transaction
    No transaction in 6 months: annual statement
    CAS monthly with a transaction; half-yearly if none; matched by PAN
    Unit certificate in 5 working days
    Portfolio statement within 10 days of half-year
    Delay interest borne by the AMC
    MITRA: tracing inactive / unclaimed folios (CAMS + KFintech)
    DigiLocker holds MF statements, with nominee access
    Voluntary debit freeze (Mar 2026), first on MF Central

@ch10|10|7|Risk, Return and Performance of Funds|Risk factors, what drives equity and debt returns, return maths, risk measures, credit events, gating and side-pocketing.
Risk, Return and Performance of Funds
  Risk factors in the SID
    Standard
      Past performance no guarantee
      Name says nothing about quality
      Sponsor liable only to initial contribution
    General
      Liquidity, interest rate, reinvestment
      Political, economic, foreign currency (AMC doesn't manage)
      Settlement / counterparty, exchange transactions
    Equity and derivatives
      Large → mid → small: more volatility and liquidity risk
      Derivatives are leveraged
      Counterparty, market liquidity, model, basis risk
      Short selling: counterparty + illiquid borrowed stock
      Securities lending: counterparty failure
    Debt
      Rating migration: AAA → AA+ = price falls
      Reinvestment, term structure, credit
      Floaters: spread and basis risk
      Corporate bond repo: counterparty risk, cut by over-collateralisation
      Money market: liquid but thin secondary market
    Securitisation
      Originator sells to an SPV (trust), which issues PTCs
      Lower LTV safer; more seasoning better; DPD buckets
      Credit enhancement: rated above originator; non-recourse
      Risks: prepayment, originator bankruptcy, co-mingling
      MFs mostly buy AA and above
  Systematic vs unsystematic
    Systematic = market, non-diversifiable (inflation)
    Unsystematic = company-specific, diversifiable (a strike)
    Investors rewarded only for systematic risk
    Manager can cut it only by holding cash
  Equity returns
    Security selection and market timing
    Fundamental: long term; technical ("chartists"): short term
    Ratios
      EPS = PAT ÷ shares
      P/E = price ÷ EPS; forward P/E uses projected EPS
      PEG: 1 fair, <1 cheap, >1 expensive
      P/BV = price ÷ book value per share
      Dividend yield = DPS ÷ price; falls in bull markets
    Growth stocks: high P/E, low yield, fall more
    Value stocks: below intrinsic value, long horizon
    EIC: Economy, Industry, Company
    Top-down (economy first) vs bottom-up (stock first)
  Debt, gold and real estate returns
    Debt: interest rates and credit spreads
    Instruments
      T-bills: RBI for GoI
      CDs: banks 7 days–1 yr; FIs 1–3 yrs
      CP: companies, up to 1 yr
      Govt bonds; company debentures
    Credit spread = corporate − gilt yield
    Raters: CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, India Ratings
    YTM if held to maturity; HPR = interest + gain / loss
    Floater: base + spread, holds value
    Rates up: shorten duration or floaters; down: go long
    9.5% coupon at 8.5% yield: premium
    Gold: global price + rupee (weaker rupee helps); passive
    Real estate: local asset; no MF schemes now; REITs growing
  Measuring returns
    Simple: 12 → 15 = 25%
    Annualised = simple × 12 ÷ months: 5% in 6 m = 10%
    CAGR = (end ÷ start)^(1/n) − 1; n = days ÷ 365
      Rs 1,000 → 4,000 in 2 yrs = 100%
      Rs 10,000 at 10% for 3 yrs = 13,310
    Dividends: reinvestment method at ex-dividend NAV
    Investor return ≠ scheme return: loads, taxes, timing
    <1 yr absolute; >1 yr CAGR
    Rolling returns remove start / end-date bias
    No promised returns except assured-return schemes (named guarantor)
  Risk from the investor's view
    Low risk ≠ zero risk: credit events in 2018 and 2019
    Redemptions raise bad paper share: 5% → 10%
    Arbitrage using views: not risk-free
    Winding-up schemes listed (Clause 37(d))
    Real estate funds less risky than direct property
  Risk measures
    Variance: fluctuation vs own average
    SD = √variance = total risk; ≥30 observations
      Annualise: ×√52 weekly, ×√12 monthly, ×√252 daily
    Beta (CAPM): systematic risk; equity only
      0.8 moves 8% when market moves 10%
    Modified duration: rate sensitivity, professionals' measure
    WAM: 70% × 4 + 30% × 1 = 3.1 yrs; for lay investors
    Credit rating: G-secs and cash have no credit risk
  Credit events, gating, side-pocketing
    Credit event = default, delay or downgrade
    Gating
      Only market-wide crises, never one bad security
      Max 10 working days in 90 days
      AMC Board + trustees; SEBI told at once
      First Rs 2 lakh of every redemption paid
    Segregated portfolio (Dec 2018)
      Optional; triggered by downgrade
      Unrated: only on actual default (7 Nov 2019)
      Trustees approve; from credit-event day; press release
      Equal units; no subscription or redemption; listed in 10 working days
      New investors get main portfolio only
      No management fee; TER only on recovery; excess legal cost on AMC
    Mitigants: hold longer; match holding period to maturity

@ch11|11|7|Mutual Fund Scheme Performance|Benchmark rules and TRI, benchmarks for every category, two-tier benchmarks, Sharpe, Treynor, alpha, information ratio and tracking error.
Mutual Fund Scheme Performance
  Benchmark rules
    Match objective, asset allocation and strategy
    Run by an independent agency, transparently
    Past returns never a basis
    Index fund benchmark = the index it tracks
    Chosen by AMC with trustees; in the SID; change justified
  TRI
    Since 1 Feb 2018 (was PRI)
    Adds dividends / interest
    Fewer schemes seem to beat it; gap ~1.5–2.5%
    Before TRI existed: composite CAGR
  Equity benchmarks
    Depends on type, universe, concentration
    Sectoral: e.g. Bankex
    Fewer stocks: Sensex 30 / Nifty 50
    Multi cap: Nifty 500 Multicap 50:25:25 / BSE 500
    Large cap: NIFTY 100 / BSE 100
    Large & mid: NIFTY LargeMidcap 250
    Mid cap: Midcap 150
    Small cap: NIFTY Smallcap 250 / BSE 250 SmallCap
    ELSS, flexi cap, dividend yield: NIFTY 500 / BSE 500
  Debt benchmarks
    From CRISIL, ICICI Securities, NSE (AMFI-recommended)
    I-Bex: Si-Bex 1–3 yrs, Mi-Bex 3–7, Li-Bex >7
    Overnight: NIFTY 1D Rate / CRISIL Liquid Overnight
    Liquid: NIFTY Liquid A-I / CRISIL Liquid Debt A-I
    Money market: Money Market A-I
    Ultra short: Ultra Short Duration Debt A-I
    Low duration: Low Duration Debt A-I
    Short duration: Short Duration Debt A-II
  Hybrid and other benchmarks
    Hybrid = blend, e.g. 65% Sensex + 35% I-Bex
    Conservative: Hybrid Composite Debt 15:85
    Balanced, BAF: 50:50
    Aggressive: 65:35
    Arbitrage: NIFTY 50 Arbitrage
    Gold ETF: price of gold
    International: local index (S&P 500, Shanghai Composite)
  Two-tier benchmarks
    SEBI Oct 2021, effective 1 Jan 2022
    Tier 1 = category; Tier 2 = manager's style
    Debt and equity schemes; all TRI
    Single benchmark: hybrid / solution, sectoral, index / ETF
    FoF: single-fund uses underlying's; multi-fund uses broad index
    Debt Tier 1 follows PRC from 1 Apr 2022
  Performance measures
    Relative return ignores risk
    Sharpe = (Rs − Rf) ÷ SD
      Total risk; (7 − 5) ÷ 0.5 = 4
      Compare like with like
    Treynor = (Rs − Rf) ÷ Beta
      Systematic risk; (8 − 5) ÷ 1.2 = 2.5
      For diversified equity
    Alpha = actual − beta-expected return; positive = outperformance
    Information Ratio
      Excess over Tier-1 ÷ SD of excess return
      Only mandatory measure; daily on AMC and AMFI sites (Jan 2025)
    Tracking error = SD of excess returns
      Index fund: ideally zero
      Active fund: lower = more consistent
    Risk-free rate = T-bill return
    "Calculations are arithmetic; evaluation is an art"
  Where to find performance
    SID: updated twice a year
    Factsheet: monthly, not mandatory; ad rules apply
    Product label: objective, asset class, horizon, risk-o-meter
    Discrete annual returns show consistency
    AMFI site; Morningstar, Value Research
    Macro: high deficit = rates up; oil moves currency

@ch12|12|15|Mutual Fund Scheme Selection|Matching funds to investors, risk ladders across categories, choosing by structure and strategy, picking within a category, options and conduct rules.
Mutual Fund Scheme Selection
  Investor fit
    Step 1: set goals
    Step 2: situation and risk appetite (need + ability + willingness)
    Growth → equity; income → debt; parking → liquid
    Equity needs a long horizon
    Age alone is a poor guide; use horizon to each goal
    Core and satellite
      Core: long-term goals (diversified, large, mid cap)
      Satellite: sector, long gilt, gold
      Moderate investor: 80% core / 20% satellite
  Risk ladders (low → high)
    Overall: liquid → debt → hybrid → equity
    Interest rate: overnight → liquid → ultra-short → low → short → medium → medium-long → long
    Credit: gilt → banking & PSU → corporate bond → credit risk
    Equity: large → large & mid → multi → mid → small
    Concentration: diversified → focused → thematic → sector
    Hybrid: arbitrage → conservative → equity savings → dynamic → multi-asset → balanced → aggressive
    Product labelling (2013): consult an adviser if unsure
  Structure and style
    Passive: no selection risk, low cost, still falls with index
    Active: higher cost; must beat benchmark
    Index or value for low-risk equity; growth for extra return
    Open-ended: liquidity at NAV less load; cash drag, flow pressure
    Close-ended: exchange exit at a discount; converges near maturity
    Close-ended can hold illiquid or long-gestation stocks
  Equity choices
    Focused: riskier than diversified, safer than sector
    Sector: timing-dependent, satellite
    Thematic (MNC, PSU, infra): between diversified and sector
    "Diversification is protection against ignorance"
    Mid / small caps shine in recoveries, fail in downturns
    Growth wins in bull markets; value in falls or long periods
    International: equity + currency; stronger dollar helps; satellite
  Debt, hybrid and gold choices
    FMP: horizon = maturity; check credit; exit via exchange
    TMFs replacing FMPs: G-secs, SDLs, AAA PSU; liquid via AMC
    Short duration: when rates expected to rise
    Liquid: savings-account alternative, up to 91 days
    Floater NAV steady; duration-managed funds volatile
    Hybrid: judge equity and debt separately
    "Conservative" refers only to equity allocation
    Be cautious with dynamic asset allocation
    Gold ETF tracks gold; gold sector fund holds miners (more volatile)
  Choosing within a category
    True to label portfolio
    Consistent outperformance, downside protection, rolling periods
    Look-back
      Equity ≥5 yrs
      Long-term debt ≥3 yrs
      Liquid: 7 days, 15 days, 1 month
    Fund age matters where returns diverge (multi cap)
    Size helps large cap; hurts mid cap and sector
    Portfolio turnover = lower of buys or sells ÷ avg net assets
      10,000 ÷ 5,000 = 200%, avg holding 6 months
      Judge against style: too short for value
    Costs matter most in debt and index funds
    Star ratings: past only, no predictive power
    Target top 3–5 (small categories) or top 10–15%
  Options
    IDCW payout: not assured; slab tax
    SWP better for regular income (capital gains tax)
    IDCW suits only tax-exempt investors
    Growth compounds pre-tax
    Book gains or losses to set off
  Do's, don'ts and conduct
    Do: suitability (PFUTP), stick to allocation, know objective
    Do: watch taxes and loads; written, consistent method
    Don't chase past performance
    Fourth Schedule: equitable treatment of all unitholders
    Social media: registered name + number on home page and every video
