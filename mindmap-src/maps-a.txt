Investment Landscape
  Goals and time value
    Financial goal = objective + amount + timeline
    Hospitalisation, theft, fire: emergency fund + insurance, not investments
    Pool approach flaw: horizon unknown
    Compounding (workbook numbers)
      Shalini: Rs 50 lakh at 8% for 10 yrs = Rs 1,07,94,625
      Rs 30,000 at 6%
        1 yr: 31,800
        10 yrs: 53,725
        20 yrs: 96,214
      Rs 10,000 at 8%
        5 yrs: 14,693
        10 yrs: 21,589
        20 yrs: 46,610
        30 yrs: 1,00,627
    Inflation erodes buying power
      100 units buys 68 after 5 yrs, 46 after 10
      Nominal = before inflation
      Real = after inflation
  Saving vs investing
    Saving precedes investing ("saving" comes from "safe")
    Top 3 evaluation factors: safety, liquidity, returns
    Exit penalty hits returns, not liquidity
    A mutual fund is a different way of investing, not a different product
  Asset classes
    Real estate
      Location is key
      Illiquid and not divisible
      Self-occupied home = expense, not investment
    Commodities
      Only class with no intermittent cash flow
      Physical gold = no regular income
      Commodity derivatives aren't investments: leveraged + short-term
    Equity
      Sensex: base 100 in 1979
      82,269 by 30 Jan 2026, ~15.4% p.a. (before dividends)
      Listed equity is liquid but unfit for short-term needs
    Fixed income
      Bond bought at issue, held to maturity: no capital gain
      Own government's bonds = safest, lowest rate
    Only financial form: equity and bonds
    Table 1.1 mapping
      Fixed income: PPF, SSY, SCSS, POMIS, endowment / money-back
      Real estate: REITs, InvITs, REMF
      Others: coins, art, stamps
  Risks
    Inflation risk
    Liquidity risk
    Credit risk
      Cause: borrower's ability or intention to pay
      Measured by rating + spreads
    Market vs specific risk
      Market-wide risk cannot be diversified
      Company / industry-specific risk can (pager = industry-specific)
    Interest rate risk
      Rates up, bond price down
      Longer maturity = bigger swing
    Managing risk
      Avoid, take a position, or diversify
      Lay investor: diversify
    Measuring volatility
      Variance, standard deviation, beta, modified duration
  Behavioural biases
    Dominant emotions: fear, greed, hope
    Loss aversion: Rs 5,000 loss hurts more than gain (Kahneman & Tversky)
    Confirmation bias: decide first, seek proof later
    Familiarity bias: "known devil vs unknown angel"
    Recency bias: bull run drives over-allocation
    Interest of the investor: IT employee holds only tech stocks
    Remedy: third opinion from an RIA or MFD
  Risk profile
    Need + ability + willingness
    Ability = finances + horizon
    Willingness = psychology
  Asset allocation
    Strategic: fixed target mix
    Tactical / dynamic
      Aims to improve risk-adjusted return
      For seasoned investors with large surplus
    Rebalancing
      Sell what rose, buy what fell
      50:50, A +20%, B −5% on Rs 1,000 each: sell Rs 125 of A
    Passive breach of scheme allocation
      Rebalance within 30 business days
      Excludes index funds / ETFs
      Not applicable to overnight funds
  DIY vs professional help
    DIY hidden costs: time + mistakes
    2% fee on Rs 10 lakh = ~Rs 20,000 a year

Concept and Role of a Mutual Fund
  Concept and role
    Invest through an MF, not in it
    Set up as a trust
    Profits and losses belong only to unitholders
    Everyone else earns a fee or commission
    Market stabiliser: MF inflows countered FPI selling in 2022 and 2025
    Each pool = a scheme with a pre-announced investment objective
    Investment policy = asset allocation + investment style (in the SID)
  Key terms
    Face value: typically Rs 10 (can be Rs 100)
    Unit capital = units × face value
    AUM = NAV × units outstanding; AMCs ranked by AUM
    NAV = net realisable value per unit if liquidated
    Mark to market: each security valued at market price daily
    IDCW = Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal (was dividend)
    Recurring expenses: % of AUM, lower the NAV
  Advantages
    Diversification from Rs 500
    Professional management and economies of scale
    Transparency: SID, SAI, KIM, portfolio, NAV (even for prospective investors)
    Tax deferral: MF pays no tax; growth option defers tax
    ELSS: Sec 80C up to Rs 1,50,000, Old Tax Regime only
    Systematic plans
      SIP: invest
      SWP: withdraw
      STP: transfer
  Limitations
    No customisation (PMS gives it)
    Choice overload (answer: SEBI categorisation)
    No control over costs
    No guaranteed returns
    Pass-through vehicle: manager controls only his own skill
  Structure
    Open-ended
      Buy / sell any time after NFO
      Unit capital keeps changing
    Close-ended
      Buy only in NFO; fixed maturity
      Compulsorily listed (except ELSS)
      Unit capital fixed
      Usually trades at a discount to NAV
    Interval fund
      Close-ended, opens at intervals; listed
      Transaction period min 2 days
      Interval period 15 days (workbook)
    ETF
      Trades all day at multiple prices
      Needs a demat account; tracks NAV closely
      Silver ETF now allowed
    Active vs passive
      Active: higher cost, aims to beat the market
      Passive / index: tracks index, low cost
    MF Lite
      Chapter X of MF Regulations 2026
      Lighter framework for passive schemes only (index, ETF, FoF)
  SEBI categories (2026)
    5 categories: Equity 13, Debt 17, Hybrid 7, Life Cycle, Other 2
    Market cap: Large 1–100, Mid 101–250, Small 251+ (full market cap)
    Equity (13)
      Multi cap: ≥75% equity, ≥25% each large, mid, small
      Large cap: 80%
      Large & Mid: 35% + 35%
      Mid cap: 65%
      Small cap: 65%
      Dividend yield: 80%
      Value: 80%
      Contra: 80% (AMC can offer both value and contra if overlap ≤50%)
      Focused: max 30 stocks, 80%
      Sectoral: 80%
      Thematic: 80% (overlap ≤50% with other schemes, except large cap)
      ELSS – Tax Saver Fund: 80% + 3-year lock-in
      Flexi cap: 65%, any mix
      Residual: money market, gold and silver, InvITs
    Debt (17)
      Overnight: 1 day (≤5% in G-secs / T-bills ≤30 days for margin)
      Liquid: up to 91 days
      Ultra-Short-Term: 3–6 months Macaulay
      Ultra Short to Short Term: 6–12 months (was low duration)
      Money market: up to 1 year
      Short Term: 1–3 yrs
      Medium Term: 3–4 yrs
      Medium to Long Term: 4–7 yrs
      Long Term: >7 yrs
      Dynamic Term: any duration
      Corporate bond: 80% in AA+ and above
      Credit risk: 65% in AA and below (AA+ excluded); no "high yield" names
      Banking and PSU Debt: 80%
      Gilt: 80% G-secs
      10-year constant maturity Gilt: 80%, duration = 10 yrs
      Floating Interest Rates Fund: 65%
      Sectoral debt: 80% in one sector, AA+ and above only
        Financial Services, Energy, Infrastructure, Housing, Real Estate
    Hybrid (7)
      Conservative: debt 75–90%, equity 10–25%
      Balanced: equity 40–60%, no arbitrage
      Aggressive: equity 65–80%
      Balanced Advantage: dynamic equity / debt
      Multi asset: ≥3 asset classes, ≥10% each (foreign not a separate class)
      Arbitrage: 65% equity; debt only G-secs <1 yr and G-sec repo
      Equity savings: 65% equity + 10% debt; net equity 15–40%
      Residual: InvITs (not arbitrage), ETCDs, gold and silver ETFs
    Life Cycle Funds
      Glide path: equity falls, debt rises near maturity
      Tenure 5–30 yrs, in multiples of 5
      Max 6 open for subscription per MF
      15-yr example (equity / debt)
        10–15 yrs left: 65–80 / 5–25
        5–10 yrs: 50–65 / 5–25
        3–5 yrs: 35–50 / 25–50
        1–3 yrs: 20–35 / 25–65
        <1 yr: 5–20 / 25–65
    Other (2)
      Index fund / ETF: 95% in index securities
      FoF: 95% in underlying fund
    One scheme per category, except
      Index funds / ETFs on different indices
      FoFs with different underlyings
      Sector / thematic on different sectors
  Other fund types
    FMP: close-ended debt; returns clearer, not guaranteed
    Target Maturity Fund: 2–14 yrs, listed, cuts reinvestment risk
    Capital protection: Rs 90 in debt grows to Rs 100, rest in equity derivatives
    IDF: close-ended, ≥90% infra debt
      IDF-MF: sponsored by banks and NBFCs
      IDF-NBFC: banks and IFCs only
    REMF: SEBI circular 2 May 2008 cities; close-ended, listed
    REITs / InvITs: not MF schemes
      Assets ≥ Rs 500 cr, offer ≥ Rs 250 cr
      Min lot Rs 10,000–15,000; traded lot 1 unit
    ESG: under thematic
      Exclusion, integration, best-in-class, impact, sustainable objectives, transition
    Smart beta: index-based, not market-cap weighted
    Quant fund: model-driven, removes the human element
  Specialised Investment Fund (SIF)
    Gap between MF and PMS
    Min Rs 10 lakh across strategies of the same AMC
    Max 25% unhedged short exposure; one strategy per category
    Equity
      Long-Short: ≥80% equity
      Ex-Top 100: ≥65%
      Sector Rotation: ≥80%, max 4 sectors
      Redemption daily or less often
    Debt (interval)
      Sectoral debt: ≥2 sectors, max 75% per sector
      Redemption weekly or less often
    Hybrid (interval)
      Hybrid Long-Short: ≥25% each in equity and debt
      Redemption twice a week or less often
  Industry growth
    AUM Rs 11.89 lakh cr (Mar 2015) → Rs 82.03 lakh cr (Feb 2026), 19.3% p.a.
    Folios 4.8 cr (2010) → 27.39 cr (Mar 2026)
    Monthly SIP Rs 8,055 cr (2019) → Rs 32,087 cr (Mar 2026)
    SIP assets Rs 15.11 lakh cr (Mar 2026)
    Household share: MF 10% → 14%; deposits 71% → 65% (2016–18)
